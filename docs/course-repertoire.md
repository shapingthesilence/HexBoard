# Course Repertoire

Built-in courses use familiar public-domain melodies when they fit the skill.
The notes are entered in [courseMelodies.ts](../web/src/learn/courseMelodies.ts)
as simple instrumental excerpts. No third-party recording, lyrics, or modern
commercial arrangement is bundled. Sources below identify the underlying music
and the public-domain editions used as references. Latest additions reviewed: 2026-11-02.

| Melody | Source and rights basis | Course treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ode to Joy | Ludwig van Beethoven (1770–1827), theme from the finale of Symphony No. 9 (1824). [Beethoven-Haus historical account](https://internet.beethoven.de/en/exhibition/beethoven-and-great-britain/) and [Mutopia public-domain score, ID 528](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=528). | Single melody transposed to C. The eight-note opening is E–E–F–G / G–F–E–D. The closing phrase adds C–C–D–E / D–C–C. First Steps uses even quarter notes and a final half note; Rhythm restores the dotted-quarter/eighth ending. These are excerpts, not the complete theme. |
| Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star | Traditional French melody, Ah! vous dirai-je, maman. [Mozarteum's K. 265 catalogue](https://kv.mozarteum.at/de/work/zwolf-variationen-in-c-uber-4057) documents Mozart's variations; [Mutopia public-domain reference, ID 2236](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=2236) identifies the theme with Twinkle. Mozart arranged the existing melody; he is not credited as its composer here. | Opening seven notes C–C–G–G–A–A–G in Movement, translated up a whole step. Rhythm adds the answer F–F–E–E–D–D–C. Major Scale reuses both phrases in C, then D. Quarter notes with half-note phrase endings. Only the melody is used, not the guitar arrangement or variations. |
| Au clair de la lune | Traditional French air. [Mutopia public-domain reference, ID 1111](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=1111), Felix Horetzky (1796–1870), *60 National Airs of Different Nations*, Boije collection #268. The reference credits Horetzky's guitar treatment; the underlying air is treated as traditional. | First four bars' top melody, lowered to C4: C–C–C–D–E–D / C–E–D–D–C. First Steps plays it without a clock. Movement translates the first six notes from C to D. Rhythm doubles the reference note values to quarters/halves and sustains the final C for four beats, replacing the reference's final rest. Major Scale recalls the excerpt and names its degrees. The guitar harmony, accompaniment, and later sections are omitted. |
| Joy to the World (ANTIOCH) | Lowell Mason (1792–1872), arranged in 1836 and published in *The Modern Psalmist* (1839), documented in the [Library of Congress historical catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/american-sheet-music-1820-to-1860/articles-and-essays/greatest-hits-1820-60-variety-music-cavalcade/1830-to-1839/). [Mutopia public-domain reference, ID 1223](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=1223) credits Handel and Mason. | Only the first eight soprano pitches, transposed from D to C: upper C–B–A–G–F–E–D–C. Used as an untimed descending-scale payoff in Major Scale; the example has equal note lengths and is explicitly a pitch demonstration. No original-rhythm assessment, lyrics, or four-part harmony is included. |
| Amazing Grace (NEW BRITAIN) | Traditional American hymn tune. First printed in shape-note collections: *Columbian Harmony* (Shaw & Spilman, 1829), then as “Harmony Grove” in *The Virginia Harmony* (1831); William Walker's *The Southern Harmony* (1835) paired it with John Newton's text. Sources: [Hymnary.org tune entry](https://hymnary.org/hymn/PsH/462) and the [Anthology of the American Hymn-Tune Repertory](https://people.bethel.edu/~rhomar/TunePages/NewBritain.html). The common modern form follows Edwin O. Excell's 1900 adaptation, also public domain by age. | First two lines of the familiar 3/4 melody, transposed to F so it stays within C4–C5: C pickup, F–A–F–A–G, F–D–C, C, F–A–F–A–G, high C. Rhythm follows the common modern form; the final C is held for one bar. Rhythm Fundamentals rehearses it without a clock, then times it with a two-beat leading rest before the pickup. No text or harmony is included. |
| Row, Row, Row Your Boat | American round. Lyrics were printed from 1852 with other tunes; the familiar melody first appears in *The Franklin Square Song Collection* (Harper & Brothers, 1881), credited to Eliphalet Oram Lyte (1842–1913), possibly as adapter rather than composer. [Historical summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Row,_Row,_Row_Your_Boat). Public domain by age. | One statement of the round in C, 6/8: C–C–C–D–E / E–D–E–F–G / twelve even eighths (high C, G, E, C, three each) / G–F–E–D–C. Played as a single line, not as a round. Rhythm Fundamentals rehearses it untimed, practices the twelve-eighth run on one pitch, then times the whole tune. No lyrics are included. |

The simplified Ode rhythm is identified to learners before its first timed
performance. Rhythm reuses the same pitches so the dotted ending changes only
one difficulty. Twinkle's answer is learned without a clock before timed use.
Familiarity is not assumed: Hear example and humming prepare an unfamiliar tune.

For future additions, record the composer/traditional attribution, original
publication or historical source, source-edition rights, and exactly what was
transposed, shortened, or rhythmically simplified. Check the specific melody
and edition rather than treating a familiar title as sufficient evidence.
Keep newer lyrics, arrangements, recordings, and translations separate from the
underlying tune. Favor a varied repertoire learners recognize and enjoy, chosen
for achievable range and musical purpose. Short original drills remain useful
for an isolated technique; they need not replace a rewarding real melody.
