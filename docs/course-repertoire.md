# Course Repertoire

Built-in courses use familiar public-domain melodies when they fit the skill.
The notes are entered in [courseMelodies.ts](../web/src/learn/courseMelodies.ts)
as simple instrumental excerpts. No third-party recording, lyrics, or modern
commercial arrangement is bundled. Sources below identify the underlying music
and the public-domain editions used as references. Source review: 2026-09-24.

| Melody | Source and rights basis | Course treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ode to Joy | Ludwig van Beethoven (1770–1827), theme from the finale of Symphony No. 9 (1824). [Beethoven-Haus historical account](https://internet.beethoven.de/en/exhibition/beethoven-and-great-britain/) and [Mutopia public-domain score, ID 528](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=528). | Single melody transposed to C. The eight-note opening is E–E–F–G / G–F–E–D. The closing phrase adds C–C–D–E / D–C–C. First Steps uses even quarter notes and a final half note; Rhythm restores the dotted-quarter/eighth ending. These are excerpts, not the complete theme. |
| Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star | Traditional French melody, Ah! vous dirai-je, maman. [Mozarteum's K. 265 catalogue](https://kv.mozarteum.at/de/work/zwolf-variationen-in-c-uber-4057) documents Mozart's variations; [Mutopia public-domain reference, ID 2236](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=2236) identifies the theme with Twinkle. Mozart arranged the existing melody; he is not credited as its composer here. | Opening seven notes C–C–G–G–A–A–G in Movement, translated up a whole step. Rhythm adds the answer F–F–E–E–D–D–C. Quarter notes with half-note phrase endings, transposed to C. Only the melody is used, not the guitar arrangement or variations. |

The simplified Ode rhythm is identified to learners before its first timed
performance. Rhythm reuses the same pitches so the dotted ending changes only
one difficulty. Twinkle's answer is learned without a clock before timed use.
Familiarity is not assumed: Hear example and humming prepare an unfamiliar tune.

For future additions, record the composer/traditional attribution, original
publication or historical source, source-edition rights, and exactly what was
transposed, shortened, or rhythmically simplified. Check the specific melody
and edition rather than treating a familiar title as sufficient evidence.
Keep newer lyrics, arrangements, recordings, and translations separate from the
underlying tune. Favor a varied repertoire learners recognize and enjoy, chosen
for achievable range and musical purpose. Short original drills remain useful
for an isolated technique; they need not replace a rewarding real melody.
